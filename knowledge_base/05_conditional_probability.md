# Conditional Probability

## Topic Area
Business Statistics - Probability Basics

## Learning Level
Beginner to Intermediate

## Concept Explanation
Conditional probability is the probability of one event happening when another event has already happened.

It answers questions such as:

What is the probability of event A happening given that event B has already occurred?

The word "given" means that we already know one condition is true.

## Key Formula
P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

## Formula Meaning
- P(A | B) means probability of A given B.
- P(A ∩ B) means probability that both A and B happen.
- P(B) means probability that B happens.
- P(B) should not be zero.

## Simple Example
Suppose there are 40 students in a class.

20 students are girls.  
10 girls like statistics.

If we know that the selected student is a girl, then the probability that she likes statistics is:

P(Likes Statistics | Girl) = 10 / 20 = 0.5

So, there is a 50% chance that a randomly selected girl likes statistics.

## Business Use Case
A business may use conditional probability to estimate:

- Probability that a customer buys a product given that the customer clicked an advertisement
- Probability that a loan defaults given that the customer has missed one payment
- Probability that a customer renews a subscription given that the customer used the product frequently

## Common Student Doubts

Q1. What does “given” mean in conditional probability?  
A1. “Given” means that one event or condition is already known to have happened.

Q2. Is P(A | B) the same as P(B | A)?  
A2. No. P(A | B) and P(B | A) are usually different.

Q3. Why do we divide by P(B)?  
A3. Because we are restricting the sample space to cases where B has already happened.

Q4. Can P(B) be zero?  
A4. No. Conditional probability is not defined if P(B) is zero.
