# Variance and Standard Deviation

## Topic Area
Business Statistics - Descriptive Statistics

## Learning Level
Beginner to Intermediate

## Concept Explanation
Variance and standard deviation measure how spread out data values are from the mean.

If data values are close to the mean, variation is low. If data values are far from the mean, variation is high.

Standard deviation is easier to interpret than variance because it is expressed in the same unit as the original data.

## Key Formula

Population variance:

σ² = Σ(x - μ)² / N

Population standard deviation:

σ = √[Σ(x - μ)² / N]

Sample variance:

s² = Σ(x - x̄)² / (n - 1)

Sample standard deviation:

s = √[Σ(x - x̄)² / (n - 1)]

## Formula Meaning
- x means each individual value.
- μ means population mean.
- x̄ means sample mean.
- N means population size.
- n means sample size.
- σ² means population variance.
- σ means population standard deviation.
- s² means sample variance.
- s means sample standard deviation.

## Simple Example
Suppose monthly sales are:

100, 100, 100

The variation is zero because all values are the same.

Now suppose monthly sales are:

50, 100, 150

The mean is still 100, but the values are more spread out. Therefore, the standard deviation is higher.

## Business Use Case
A company can use standard deviation to understand whether sales are stable or unpredictable. Low standard deviation means performance is consistent. High standard deviation means performance is fluctuating.

## Common Student Doubts

Q1. What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?  
A1. Variance is the average squared deviation from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of variance and is easier to understand because it uses the original unit.

Q2. Why do we square the deviations?  
A2. We square deviations to avoid positive and negative differences cancelling each other out.

Q3. What does high standard deviation mean?  
A3. It means the data values are widely spread from the mean.

Q4. What does low standard deviation mean?  
A4. It means the data values are close to the mean.
