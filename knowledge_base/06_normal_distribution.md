# Normal Distribution

## Topic Area
Business Statistics - Probability Distributions

## Learning Level
Beginner to Intermediate

## Concept Explanation
Normal distribution is a continuous probability distribution that is bell-shaped and symmetrical around the mean.

In a normal distribution, most values are close to the mean, and fewer values are far away from the mean.

Many real-world measurements are approximately normal, such as heights, test scores, measurement errors, and demand variations.

## Key Properties
- The curve is bell-shaped.
- The distribution is symmetrical.
- Mean, median, and mode are equal.
- Most observations are near the mean.
- Fewer observations are far from the mean.
- The spread of the curve depends on the standard deviation.

## Key Formula
Normal distribution is often written as:

X ~ N(μ, σ²)

## Formula Meaning
- X means the variable being studied.
- μ means the mean.
- σ² means the variance.
- σ means the standard deviation.

## Simple Example
Suppose the average marks in a class are 70, and the standard deviation is 10.

If marks are normally distributed, most students will score near 70. Fewer students will score very low or very high.

## Business Use Case
Businesses use normal distribution to:

- Forecast demand
- Analyze sales variation
- Study quality control defects
- Understand customer behavior patterns
- Estimate delivery time variation
- Analyze process performance

## Common Student Doubts

Q1. Why is it called bell-shaped?  
A1. Because the graph looks like a bell, with most values near the center and fewer values at the extremes.

Q2. Are all datasets normally distributed?  
A2. No. Some datasets are skewed, irregular, or have extreme outliers.

Q3. What does standard deviation do in normal distribution?  
A3. Standard deviation controls how spread out the curve is.

Q4. What happens when standard deviation is small?  
A4. The curve becomes narrower because values are close to the mean.

Q5. What happens when standard deviation is large?  
A5. The curve becomes wider because values are more spread out.
